The 300% Problem

Fourteen strategies, 9 years of tick-clean Binance data, and a target of 300% net annual profit at under 20% drawdown. This is the record of what was tested, what survived, and the arithmetic that says the target itself is out of reach.

Verdict

No strategy qualified.

The best configuration found clears four of the five numeric gates — 622 trades, profit factor 1.29, maximum drawdown 16.2%, realistic sizing — and misses the profit gate by a factor of about seven and a half. Nothing in the search came close, and section 7 shows why nothing was going to.

The drawdown pass is thinner than it looks. A stationary block bootstrap of the same daily returns (1,500 resamples, 5-day blocks) puts the median maximum drawdown at 17.4% and the 5th percentile at 27.1% — a 29% chance of breaching the 20% limit on a different draw of the same process. The realised 16.2% is a favourable sample, not a guarantee.

Read this honestly: BTC buy & hold ends higher than every strategy here. Over this particular window bitcoin rose about eleven-fold, and no risk-controlled system beat that in absolute terms. What the portfolio buys is the shape of the line, not its endpoint — a 16.2% worst drawdown against buy & hold's 77.2%, and a Sharpe ratio of 1.93 against 0.90. That is a real and useful difference, and it is not the same thing as making more money.

S5 · Dynamic funding carry (delta-neutral)

RANK 6 · CAGR 15.4% · DD −3.9% · Sharpe 6.72 · N 29

Long spot, short perpetual, equal size. Delta is roughly zero; the return is the funding the short leg receives, plus the change in basis, minus four legs of cost per round trip.

Legs
Long spot BTCUSDT, short perp BTCUSDT, equal BTC quantity
Entry
Trailing mean of the last 9 funding settlements > 0.8 bps per 8h
Exit
That mean falls back below the threshold. Reverse carry disabled in the ranked variant.
Capital
The spot leg consumes full notional; the perp short is margined at 5×. Deployable notional per unit of equity = 1 ÷ (1 + 1/5) = 0.83.
Funding
Real Binance settlements at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC on open notional
Costs
8 bps per leg per side (four legs per round trip); USDT borrow at 8% APR on the levered portion; spot borrow 3% APR when running the reverse carry

Two corrections moved this result a long way. Realising the basis P&L at unwind, and charging the USDT borrow on the levered spot leg, together cut the 3× variant from 41.5% to 29.0% CAGR. A carry backtest that skips the borrow cost is not a carry backtest.

6 — The caveat that matters most

The carry premium is decaying

Risk parity puts 47.8% of the portfolio's capital in the delta-neutral carry sleeve, because it has the highest Sharpe and almost no correlation with the rest. That allocation is doing most of the work in the headline number — and the premium it harvests has fallen by an order of magnitude over the test window.

Half-year2020 H12021 H12021 H22022 H2 20232024 H12025 H12025 H22026
Annualised funding run-rate 23.4%42.0% 18.3%4.2%5.5%17.2% 9.0%5.3% 3.0%

At a 3% funding run-rate against an 8% APR USDT borrow, levered carry is unprofitable today. The historical contribution of that sleeve is not repeatable, which means the portfolio's forward-looking Sharpe is much closer to its out-of-sample figure of 1.51 than to the 3.27 measured across the full sample. Anyone reading the 39.5% as a forecast is reading it wrong.

7 — Why the target is out of reach

The arithmetic behind the shortfall

The gap is not a matter of finding a better indicator. Two independent calculations put the required skill an order of magnitude above anything a single instrument can supply.

Route one — the drawdown constraint fixes the ratio

300% annual return ÷ 20% maximum drawdown = Calmar ratio ≥ 15

Across every portfolio size tested, the measured relationship was Calmar ≈ 1.3 × Sharpe (size 1: 3.27 → 4.73; size 6: 1.93 → 2.45; size 8: 1.82 → 2.37). Leverage moves return and drawdown together and leaves the ratio roughly where it was — which is exactly why raising the knob from 1 to 8 never got close. A Calmar of 15 therefore needs a Sharpe ratio around 11, net of costs. The best full-sample Sharpe in this study was 3.27, and out of sample it was 1.51.

Route two — the signal-strength ceiling

Sharpe ≈ IC × √(independent bets per year)

The strongest predictor found in the entire search was top-trader-versus-retail positioning, at a rank-IC of 0.108 against 96-hour forward returns. A 96-hour horizon on one instrument gives about 8,766 ÷ 96 ≈ 91 non-overlapping bets a year, so 0.108 × √91 ≈ 1.03 — which is almost exactly the Sharpe of 1.13 that strategy actually delivered. The model is not being pessimistic; it is describing what happened.

Reaching Sharpe 11 at that same IC would need (11 ÷ 0.108)² ≈ 10,400 independent bets a year. One asset offers 91 at this horizon. Shortening the horizon to buy breadth does not work either: the same feature family measured IC 0.013 at one hour, and the 16 bps round-turn eats everything below roughly a 24-hour holding period.

What the gap would actually take

Breadth of that order comes from one of two places: several hundred weakly-correlated instruments traded as a cross-section, or sub-second market making where the edge is the spread and the bet count runs to millions a year. The first is outside the brief, which specifies BTCUSDT. The second cannot be evaluated from OHLCV data at all — it needs full order-book reconstruction and a queue-position model, and its returns are a function of latency and fee tier rather than of any signal.

Within the brief as written — one instrument, honest costs, real funding, no look-ahead — 300% net annual at under 20% drawdown does not appear to be attainable. The evidence here is that the ceiling sits near 40%.

Look-ahead controls

  • Indicators for bar t use only bars ≤ t. A signal formed at the close of a 4h bar is executed at the open of the next 15-minute bar, never earlier.
  • Stops and targets are resolved on 15-minute bars rather than guessed on the decision bar. Where a single 15-minute bar spans both levels, the stop is assumed to fill first.
  • The order-flow orthogonalisation refits monthly on an expanding window of strictly past data. A full-sample regression here would have leaked, and did in the first draft.
  • Machine-learning walk-forward uses a purge gap equal to the label horizon, so no training label overlaps the block being predicted.
  • Portfolio weights are estimated on the in-sample window alone and held fixed across the out-of-sample period.
  • Funding and positioning observations are keyed to the moment they became public, not the bar they describe.
