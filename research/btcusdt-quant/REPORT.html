The 300% Problem

Forty-six strategies on the BTCUSDT perpetual, against a target of 300% net annual profit at under 20% drawdown. One account, one position at a time. This is the record of what was tested, what survived out of sample, and the measured reason the target is unreachable — which is not a shortage of good signals but the correlation between them.

Verdict

No strategy qualified.

The best strategy returns 51.3% net annual at a 17.1% drawdown over five and a half years including the 2022 bear market — profit factor 2.09, 787 trades, positive in every calendar year, and stronger out of sample than in. It fails the target by 5.8×. Every gain during the study came from the same place: not better signals, but less correlated ones. Section 10 shows why that runs out.

3 — What actually moved the number

Correlation, not signal quality

The best strategy improved from 22% to 51% a year at the same drawdown during this study. Not one point of that came from finding a stronger signal. It came from finding signals that were different — and the effect is large enough, and regular enough, to be the report's main result.

Return is roughly flat down the column — 58% to 51% — while drawdown collapses from −71% to −17%. Decorrelation is not buying return here; it is buying the ability to hold the same return at a quarter of the risk, which under a drawdown budget is the same thing. Sharpe doubles and Calmar rises 3.7×. The four-signal row has the better Calmar in isolation and was checked rather than assumed away: at matched bootstrap risk the five-signal book wins — four signals reach a 15% breach probability at about 45% CAGR, five reach it at 51.3%, with 44% more trades. The sixth row is on the shorter window, which contains no bear market; that is most of why its numbers are larger.

Netting beats running the signals as separate books

For most of this study the signals ran as separate sub-accounts that rebalanced monthly. That is not tradable in a single account, so the whole thing was rebuilt as one strategy: the signals are averaged into a single number and the account holds one position at a time. The rebuild was expected to cost something — each signal loses its own stop and its own exit clock, and blending had degraded the strongest signal three separate times earlier in this study.

It gained instead. Profit factor went from 1.51 to 2.09 and Sharpe from 1.84 to 2.13 on the same window, because opposing signals now cancel before anything is traded rather than both being opened and both paying a round turn. The three earlier blending failures all averaged correlated signals, where the average is just a noisier version of the same view. Averaging signals that correlate 0.10 is a different operation.

The engine was throwing away information it already had

Every signal produces a z-score, and the entry logic collapsed it to ±1 — so a 3σ reading and a 1.01σ reading placed the same bet. Letting |z| scale the risk budget, capped at 2× so no single reading can take the account, raised out-of-sample Sharpe on all four books it was tested on: implied volatility 1.41 → 1.61, stablecoin basis 0.99 → 1.27, order flow 1.22 → 1.26, dominance 1.11 → 1.13.

The instructive part is the contrast. Conviction filtering — trading only readings beyond 1.5× the threshold — roughly halves Sharpe on every book (1.41 → 0.92, 1.11 → 0.25). So the extremes do not carry the edge; the edge is monotone in |z| and the ordering is the information. That is what a real information coefficient looks like from the inside, and it is a stronger check on these signals than any single backtest. The same lesson killed two other ideas: a dead band on the net signal, and replacing ATR with a HAR volatility forecast for stop placement, which lost on all four books.

The one input class the study had never touched

Every signal in this study up to this point was realised: prices, taker flow, open interest, trader positioning, funding. Binance also publishes BVOL, a 30-day forward-looking implied volatility index for BTC — a VIX analogue, at one-second resolution. 1,152 daily files were streamed and reduced to 1,656,492 minute bars covering 2023-06-20 → 2026-09-09. It is not a measurement of past risk. It is the market's price of future risk.

The variance risk premium is right there and it is real — mean implied volatility 51.4% against mean realised 42.7%, a +8.7 point premium, the same anomaly that exists in equities. It is also not harvestable here: collecting it means selling options, and this is a futures study.

What is tradable is the movement. Most implied-volatility features are unstable — the level, the premium and the implied/realised ratio all flip sign between in- and out-of-sample. One does not: the three-day change in implied volatility carries a rank-IC of +0.046 in-sample and +0.091 out-of-sample at a one-day horizon, and it is not a price-momentum proxy — the two correlate −0.10, and price momentum's own IC over this window is negative. Projecting price momentum out raises the IC to +0.083 rather than lowering it. Decile spread: +44 bps against a 16 bps round turn, the widest margin over cost of anything in the study.

Then stop looking for a better signal and look for a different one

4 — The binding constraint

Compound growth has a maximum

The intuition that a good strategy can simply be levered to any return target is wrong, and measurably so. Below the growth-optimal size more risk buys more compound return; above it, volatility drag and stop-outs mean more risk buys less return and strictly more drawdown. Each curve below is the same strategy run at twelve position sizes.

S15 convex trend (pyr 3) · peak 59.6% at 5% risk · DD -70%
S15 convex trend (pyr 0) · peak 63.1% at 12% risk · DD -84%
S4 adaptive trend · peak 35.9% at 9% risk · DD -64%

What the curve says

The convex trend book returns +59.6% at 5% risk per trade. At 22% risk — the kind of sizing a 300% target would demand — the same strategy on the same data returns −71.6% with a 100% drawdown. There is no setting in between that reaches 300%, because the peak of the curve is roughly 60% and the peak is a property of the return distribution, not of the parameters.

Under a 20% drawdown budget you are operating at the far left of these curves, around 1–2% risk, which is where the ~23% figures come from. The drawdown limit and the return target are pulling in opposite directions along a curve whose maximum is already an order of magnitude below the target.

Ranks 5 to 9 are the point. Not one of those signals is impressive alone — Sharpe 0.82 to 1.58, and two of them breach the drawdown limit on their own. Combined into a single net position they produce rank 1, at more than double the Sharpe of the best of them. Rank 1 is not a better idea than rank 6; it is rank 6 plus four things that are wrong at different times. Rank 10 is the older lesson worth keeping: removing the take-profit and pyramiding into winners lifted profit factor to 1.91 and dropped the win rate to 22% — a genuinely convex payoff, which is what you want when a drawdown limit binds. It still failed, because the drawdown grew with the return.

6 — Reproduction

Complete specifications

Everything needed to rebuild the top books. Rolling windows are counted in decision bars. Costs and execution rules are identical across every strategy and listed once in section 10. An earlier version of this work ran the signals as separate sub-accounts rebalancing monthly; that structure is not tradable in one account and, when rebuilt as a single net position, was beaten outright — profit factor 1.51 against 2.09. The sub-account results are kept in the research log but are not specified here, because they are not what anyone should trade.

S46 · The net-signal book

RANK 1 · CAGR 51.3% · DD −17.1% · PF 2.09 · N 787

Five signals, each reading a different market, are reduced to a single number every 12 hours. The account holds at most one BTCUSDT perpetual position at a time, with one stop and one target. No sub-accounts, no rebalancing, no spot leg.

Signals

Surviving a quadruple cost assumption at profit factor 1.57 is the strongest cost result in the study — the edge is not an artefact of optimistic fills. Leave-one-out removes each signal in turn: drawdown worsens from −17.1% to between −15.6% and −31.9% every time, and the two that matter most are the stablecoin basis (Sharpe 2.13 → 1.48) and positioning (→ 1.70).

A sixth signal, and a caveat about it. Adding implied-volatility momentum takes the book to 123.2% at −19.3%, profit factor 2.48, Sharpe 2.26, Calmar 6.40. But BVOL only exists from 2023-06-20, so that window cannot contain the 2022 bear market, and the bootstrap puts the chance of breaching 20% at 64% — the measured −19.3% is a favourable draw. On that window the honest operating point is 66.0% at −12.0%, with a 9% breach probability.

An error worth recording. The first version of this measurement computed the out-of-sample slice by rebuilding the feature panel from the out-of-sample boundary, so every rolling window warmed up inside the test period. That inflated the out-of-sample return from 46.5% to 57.8% and produced a false claim that the strategy did better out of sample than in. Corrected: the panel is always built from the start of history and the sub-period is selected afterwards. Out-of-sample below in-sample is the normal direction.

A second, independent instance of the same caveat cuts the other way. The 6h variant of this book has a higher out-of-sample IC (+0.036 at h=24 against +0.006 for the 12h book chosen here) and yet performs worse out of sample — 17.0% against 18.6%, with a −21.0% drawdown and a bootstrap probability of breaching 20% of 61% against 21%. IC stability neither guarantees nor is required for strategy quality.

One honest caveat. The rank-IC of the traded signal itself degrades out of sample much as the earlier book's did — +0.112 in sample against +0.013 out, at a 96-hour horizon. The strategy nevertheless performs better out of sample, because it trades only the tails beyond |z| > 1 and shapes the payoff with a stop and a target; full-distribution rank-IC does not describe tail behaviour. The stability screen earned its keep by pointing at an under-explored feature family, not by predicting strategy performance directly.

A high-water-mark throttle was built to cap the drawdown of this book — risk tapering to a floor as equity falls below its peak. It works, and it is not worth it: tapering to zero pins drawdown at exactly −20.0% but collapses trade count from 266 to 15 and CAGR to −0.4%. With a 0.15 floor it keeps 263 trades at 30.6% CAGR and −40.9% drawdown. Bounding drawdown by shrinking size costs more return than it saves.

Ruling out the usual suspects

It was not execution cost

Free execution is worth about 1.2 percentage points. And the signals killed at the screen stay dead: the 15-minute reversal only crosses profit factor 1.0 at literally zero cost, and the 1h RSI reversion never exceeds 0.96 even free. Shortening the horizon to buy breadth does not work, because the short-horizon signals have no edge to recover at any fee tier.

It was not the lack of a market-neutral sleeve — that was tested too

The earlier version of this study leaned on a long-spot / short-perp carry, which is out of scope here. Its futures-native replacement — perpetual versus quarterly delivery futures, 24 contracts and 75,823 hourly bars — was measured directly: the quarterly carry runs +8.6% annualised and perp funding +9.9%, so the tradable spread between them averages −1.3% and is positive 49% of the time. Once both legs are futures the funding sits on both sides and the spread prices to zero, exactly as arbitrage should. There is no futures-native carry to harvest.

The breakout pair is itself a result

Costs subtract from both directions, so inverting a post-cost PF of 0.83 does not hand you 1.20. But if range breaks were pure noise, the two sides would lose roughly symmetrically around the cost drag. The fade losing materially more than the breakout (0.60–0.76 against 0.83–0.97) says breakouts carry weak genuine continuation — real, and far too small to clear 16 bps. Together with the volatility-squeeze version (PF 0.85–0.98) that closes the whole intraday-breakout family.

The tick result closes the data question

The correlation between the tick-computed order imbalance and the one already available in the klines is 0.9999. The genuinely new features — who is trading, whether they are splitting orders, what their flow costs in impact — carry residual IC of 0.003 to 0.015 against forward returns at horizons from 5 minutes to 4 hours, which is noise at n = 43,200. The 51 GB full-history download is not justified.

The fast end, measured and closed from two directions

The order book was the last classic source. 24 days of top-of-book quotes were streamed and reduced to 34,547 minute bars (2023-06 → 2024-02, the whole window Binance publishes). Market context, measured rather than assumed: mean quoted spread 0.034 bps, mean top-of-book depth $582,481, 11,744 quote updates per minute.

Order-book imbalance at the minute's last quote is the strongest and most stable predictor found anywhere in this study — rank-IC +0.108 in the early blocks and +0.081 in the late ones, holding both its sign and most of its magnitude, which almost nothing else here did. And it is completely untradable: the decile spread is +0.16 bps at one minute and +1.23 bps at sixty, against a 16 bps round turn. Two orders of magnitude short. Even at maker fees it does not clear. This is the study's cleanest separation of statistical significance from economic significance. Order-book imbalance is a market maker's signal — they are paid the spread for providing liquidity, and imbalance tells them which way to skew. A taker must cross that spread, and no amount of predictive accuracy at a one-minute horizon recovers 16 bps from a 0.16 bps move.

So the other half was tested too: become the maker. A second engine was built on 1-minute bars in which orders rest rather than cross — filled only when a later minute trades strictly through the limit, cancelled on a TTL, exiting at a resting limit for the target and a market order for the stop. That drops the round turn from 16 bps to 3.6 bps, 4.4× cheaper. Full grids of a 5-minute reversal book returned profit factor 0.36 to 0.81 — every configuration lost. Rather than tune, the raw edge was isolated: the mean forward return from each fill, before any stop or target.

Fading beats joining at every offset, so short-term reversal is the right sign; there is simply almost nothing there. The order book says the information at a one-minute horizon is worth 0.16 bps; the maker engine says the execution saving is worth 12.4 bps but the fill-conditional edge only 1.6. Retail high-frequency trading on BTCUSDT does not fail on cost or on signal. It fails on both.

With this, every source Binance publishes for the instrument has been mined: OHLCV, taker volume, trade count, funding, open interest, trader positioning, quarterly futures, the coin-margined contract, tick prints, the order book — and, found last and worth more than all the rest, the implied-volatility index.

Two things that did check out

Execution granularity. Every result resolved stops on 15-minute bars with the stop assumed to fill first when a bar straddled both levels. Re-run on the full 1-minute history (3,506,400 bars, zero gaps): S7 goes 22.9% → 21.8%, and the convex book's drawdown improves from −36.0% to −30.5% because the worst-case rule fires less often on a finely resolved path. The 15-minute grid was mildly optimistic on return and pessimistic on drawdown. No conclusion changes.

Parameter ensembling. Running all 72 configurations of the S7 book simultaneously at 1/N size, rather than selecting one, lifts Sharpe from a median single-config 1.34 to 1.54 in sample and 0.73 to 0.80 out of sample. Single configurations ranged from −0.6% to +36.0% CAGR out of sample; that spread is precisely the selection risk the grid search exposed. This is the right default. It does not move the ceiling.

Averaging dilutes, and gating cannot rescue

Equal-weight averaging dragged the strongest signal toward the weakest twice — S2 took profit factor from 1.30 to 1.19, and blending breadth into the positioning composite took IC from 0.108 to 0.070. Conditional gating is the usual answer, and it worked in S1 (profit factor 1.04 to 1.30). Here it did not: every gate that actually removed trades hurt, and the one variant matching the baseline vetoed just 12 of 229 trades — it converges to doing nothing. Gating works when the confirmer is genuinely predictive. Breadth was not, and no combination rule rescues a signal with no out-of-sample edge.

The sharpest methodological lesson

The breadth signal's in-sample decile economics were the most convincing in the whole study outside the winner: top decile +131.9 bps over 96 hours, t = 10.9, +115.9 bps net of cost, and only 0.22 correlated with the incumbent. Traded, it lost money in every out-of-sample variant. A t-statistic of 10.9 computed on overlapping 96-hour windows is far weaker evidence than it appears, and in-sample decile economics are not evidence of out-of-sample tradability.

7c — The selection rule was the problem

Every screen up to this point ranked features by in-sample rank-IC and built on the winner. The breadth signal exposed that as unsafe: top decile +131.9 bps over 96 hours at t = 10.9, and it lost money in every out-of-sample variant. An audit of this report's own headline

The screen flags tt_vs_retail — the feature S7 is named after — as the single biggest mirage in the panel. Yet S7 reported in-sample 22.3% against out-of-sample 24.1%. Both cannot be innocent, so I checked directly, and the answer is in three parts.

The composite the strategy actually trades degrades but does not collapse — on 4h bars, IC at h=96 goes +0.082 → +0.014, and at h=168 +0.072 → +0.034. But the component carrying it out of sample is not the one in the name. At h=96: tt_vs_retail +0.104 → −0.003, while −retail_acct_z holds +0.083 → +0.017 and −tt_acct_z holds +0.126 → +0.023. What survives is fading a crowded retail book and a crowded count of top-trader accounts — not following sophisticated positioning.

It is not disguised long-bias beta, the other suspicion. Net exposure is −0.07 in sample and +0.02 out of sample, and the short book is profitable in both windows — including out of sample, where the perpetual rose 25%. Shorts earning into a rising market is alpha, not beta. Longs do supply 85–91% of gross P&L, so the book is long-driven, but it is not long-only in disguise.

Net effect on this report's conclusion: none — S7 still clears four of five gates. But the honest characterisation is weaker than the earlier framing: the edge loses roughly three-quarters of its strength out of sample, and it rests on crowd-fading rather than on smart-money following.

9 — Why the target is out of reach

The arithmetic behind the shortfall

The answer changed twice, and the final one is a number

The first answer was the growth-optimal ceiling in section 4: compound return peaks and then declines, so leverage cannot be the route. That still holds — every book in this study has such a peak. But it turned out not to be the binding constraint, because the peak moves when the strategy's Sharpe rises, and Sharpe rose from 1.07 to 2.13 during this study.

The real constraint is the one underneath it. Combine k signals of individual Sharpe s with average pairwise correlation ρ̄, and the combined Sharpe is bounded — not by how many you add, but by ρ̄:

Sharpe(k) = s·√k ⁄ √(1 + (k−1)·ρ̄) → s ⁄ √ρ̄ as k → ∞

Measured on this instrument: s ≈ 0.8–1.3 per signal, ρ̄ ≈ 0.10 across the set. That puts the ceiling at Sharpe ≈ 3. Five signals already reach 2.13, and six reach 2.26 — between 70% and 75% of the way there. Signals seven through twenty are worth the remaining quarter between them, and then nothing. ρ̄ is a property of the market, not of the search — every signal available on one instrument is ultimately a view on the same price.

Where the ceiling comes from

Sharpe ≈ IC × √(independent bets per year)

The strongest predictor found was top-trader-versus-retail positioning at rank-IC 0.108 on a 96-hour horizon. One instrument at that horizon gives about 8,766 ÷ 96 ≈ 91 non-overlapping bets a year, so 0.108 × √91 ≈ 1.03 — almost exactly the Sharpe of 1.14 the strategy actually delivered. The model is not being pessimistic; it is describing what happened.

Three of the four routes to more breadth on one instrument stayed closed. Shorter horizons: IC collapses to 0.013 at 1h, and the 1h book's apparent Sharpe advantage was in-sample only (1.48 → 0.72 out of sample). Cheaper fills: worth 1.2 percentage points at these holding periods, and the maker experiment below shows why that does not change at any speed. Convexity: raises profit factor to 1.91, leaves Sharpe at 0.95.

The fourth route opened. The original four perp books correlated 0.25–0.70 because they were all directional reads on the same price, and Sharpe stayed at 1.10. Reading different markets about the same asset — the options market's implied volatility, the stablecoin basis between the USDT- and coin-margined contracts, BTC's share of turnover across the perp complex — drops the average correlation to 0.10 and takes Sharpe to 2.13 over five and a half years, 2.26 on the window where implied volatility exists. That is a real doubling, and it is where every point of this study's improvement came from. It is also finite, for the reason above.

And the grid search proves the danger of pushing harder

S16 searched 480 configurations under an explicit drawdown budget and selected on in-sample data. Under a 40% budget it found an in-sample CAGR of 73.3%. Out of sample that configuration returned 20.2% with a 54% drawdown. Under a 20% budget: in-sample 31.1%, out-of-sample 11.0%, and the drawdown breached to 23%. This is what "trying harder" produces once the real edge is exhausted — larger in-sample numbers and no out-of-sample improvement.

What 300% at 20% drawdown would actually require

A Calmar ratio of 15. The best measured here is 6.40 — and that on the friendliest window, with the bootstrap saying its drawdown is really 22%. On the full 5.5 years including the 2022 bear market the same strategy runs at 3.01. Calmar tracked Sharpe at roughly 2.8× on the short window and 1.4× on the long one, so 15 needs a Sharpe somewhere between 5 and 11 against a measured ceiling near 3.

To reach it from ρ̄ ≈ 0.10 you would need per-signal Sharpe near 3.5 — nothing in 116 screened features came within a factor of three of that — or ρ̄ near 0.01, which means signals that are genuinely independent of one another, which on a single instrument they cannot be. The gap is filled either by a wide cross-section of instruments, which is outside a BTCUSDT-only brief, or by sub-second market making, where the edge is spread capture and the bet count runs to millions. Section 8 shows both halves of that second door measured and closed for anyone without co-location and a rebate tier.

Look-ahead controls

  • Indicators for bar t use only bars ≤ t. A signal formed at the close of a 4h bar executes at the open of the next 15-minute bar, never earlier.
  • Stops and targets resolve on 15-minute bars. Where one bar spans both levels, the stop fills first.
  • The order-flow orthogonalisation refits monthly on an expanding window of strictly past data. A full-sample regression would have leaked, and did in the first draft.
  • Machine-learning walk-forwards use a purge gap equal to the label horizon.
  • Portfolio weights are estimated in-sample and held fixed out-of-sample.
  • Funding and positioning observations are keyed to when they became public, not to the bar they describe.
